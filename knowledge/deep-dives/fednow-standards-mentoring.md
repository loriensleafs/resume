## Problem

Code reviews across FedNow's teams kept finding the same kinds of problems. Test coverage was low, and front-end tests were hard to write. Many developers needed support with modern React and front-end patterns, and teams had no shared place to bring front-end questions.

## What Peter did

Peter wrote and introduced front-end standards and best practices that 5+ teams adopted, including standardized lint rules and patterns for form state. He developed a testing harness that, according to his 2024 resume, simplified test writing for developers; he is confirming its details with a former colleague, so the chat has no further detail about it. He mentored 20+ developers one-on-one across teams, on building with the new architecture, the design system and the shared packages; on modern React; on code review practice; and on career growth. He also ran weekly office hours open to every development team.

## How it worked

The standards were documented in detail in Confluence and backed by standardized lint rules. Because the architecture, packages and patterns were standardized, the weekly office hours could troubleshoot any team's problems quickly, and the same documentation served every team.

## Result

The standards reduced code review issues across the teams that adopted them. According to Peter's 2024 resume, the testing harness increased test coverage across all applications in the organization. Other teams ramped up much more quickly and learned how to build FedNow's more complex front ends.

## Tools

Confluence for the standards and documentation, and standardized lint rules. Details of the testing harness's tools are being confirmed.

## Not public

FedNow's security controls, infrastructure and environments, and anything about how FedNow is protected. The names and internals of FedNow applications, internal repositories and code. The names of colleagues and the Federal Reserve's internal team structure.
